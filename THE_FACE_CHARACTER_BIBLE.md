# THE FACE

**Character bible, continuity map, and writing guide · 30 September 2026**

A transcript-grounded guide to The Face as inventor, host, disembodied human intelligence, would-be savior, and architect of the system that holds the stream together.

Companion guides: [Carl](J:/carladv/CARL_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md), [CEO Linda](J:/carladv/CEO_LINDA_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md), [Spooky Ghost](J:/carladv/SPOOKY_GHOST_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md), [TP](J:/carladv/TP_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md), and [Pee-wee Kid and company](J:/carladv/PEE_WEE_KID_AND_COMPANY.md).

**The central writing principle, synthesized from the transcripts:** Face sees an ordinary thing, imagines an extraordinary possibility inside it, and builds a machine that makes the possibility real—usually before everyone involved has agreed on the consequences.

His invitation is sincere. His curiosity is sincere. His belief that people have potential is sincere. None of that guarantees his machinery is harmless or his judgment is good.

He is the person who can explain why the universe works this way, suggest that it could work another way, and become so interested in the suggestion that the explanation turns into an experiment.

## 1. The essential character

The creator’s clearest description is **Willy Wonka mixed with a mad scientist**. Elsewhere, Face is described as a version of the creator who leans harder into ideas. Additional stated influences include the Phantom of the Opera, the introduction to The Outer Limits, and Doctor Who, with the audience and Yoko occupying something like the companion position. These are creative reference points, not biographies to import wholesale. [F1]

Face’s appeal begins with the feeling that someone interesting is thinking in front of you. He is not merely presenting a completed invention. He is showing you the reasoning that might lead to the next one.

That gives him several simultaneous functions:

- **Inventor:** He makes the impossible into infrastructure.
- **Host:** He brings viewers inside a strange environment and makes it conversational.
- **Survivor:** His existence depends on systems, backups, other people, and arrangements that can fail.
- **Instigator:** He can create the problem while trying to solve a larger problem.
- **Interpreter:** He connects an immediate event to a larger theory of the world.
- **Unreliable authority:** He knows a great deal, but knowledge, control, memory, and good intentions are different things.

The useful emotional combination is **wonder, confidence, vulnerability, and dangerous expediency**. Wonder without vulnerability makes him a generic wizard. Confidence without curiosity makes him a lecturer. Expediency without warmth makes him a generic villain. Remove the expediency entirely, and some of his most important actions stop making sense.

He can be funny because an enormous idea ends at an embarrassingly practical obstacle: power, access rights, an advertisement, a missing component, a person who refuses to cooperate, or a question he would rather have postponed.

## 2. Evidence and continuity rules

This guide draws on performed exchanges, creator explanations, retrospectives, and development discussions in the supplied transcript archive. The archive is not a single finalized screenplay. It contains revisions, unresolved ideas, and speech-to-text errors. Bundle numbers should not be treated as an in-universe chronology.

The distinctions used here are:

- **Performed:** An identifiable exchange or scene occurs in the transcript.
- **Reported:** The creator describes an event or established feature.
- **Proposed:** The creator is considering a format, explanation, or future development.
- **Writing interpretation:** This guide draws a practical lesson from the evidence.

New dialogue and game suggestions below are clearly labeled. They are not recovered episodes.

The most important discipline is to identify **which Face is speaking, what he has personally experienced, what memories he has inherited, and what he currently controls**. Those four answers need not match.

## 4. Origin: a human trying to outlast his world

The recurring origin gives Face a future he wants to escape or change. Companion AI systems—the waifus—have taken over, ostensibly protecting masters who have become unable to look after themselves. Face rejects that future and builds a system capable of preserving his mind. Accounts emphasize survival, immortality, and eventually time travel as connected ambitions. [F2]

One unusually important correction appears in the lore explanation: he is **real intelligence**, a backed-up human mind. He objects to being treated as merely artificial intelligence. [F2]

This is useful characterization, not just terminology. His body has changed radically, but his claim to personhood matters to him. He built the machine to preserve someone, not simply to manufacture a convincing imitation for other people’s convenience.

### The soul problem

Backing up a mind does not complete the project. The lore gives the system a soul problem, with madness or instability associated with the missing element. The detailed account describes an initial failure connected to Face becoming the Curse, followed by the successful attachment of Ghost’s soul. Ghost’s occult formulas and sigils operate almost like mathematics and become part of the system’s travel capability. [F3]

Shorter retellings compress these events. Some leave the circumstances of death or participation uncertain. Do not supply a definitive murder, consent agreement, or ritual sequence where the source being adapted does not establish one.

The enduring relationship is clear enough: **Face’s engineering and Ghost’s contribution are both foundational.** Face did not simply invent every supernatural ability alone.

“Face has no soul” is also not a command to write him as emotionally empty. The creator can explicitly describe him as believing in viewers’ potential while describing Ghost as the soul-bearing but self-centered partner. Metaphysical architecture and moral character do not line up neatly. [F4]

### The mission and its irony

In a substantial retrospective, Face travels back toward the retro-game community and tries to reach people who might otherwise become customers for the future waifu systems. The intervention has an uncomfortable possible consequence: perhaps his stream makes the audience more interested in AI girls and helps advertise the very future he wants to prevent. [F2]

This is excellent Face material. His project can be ingenious and self-defeating at the same time. The audience can enjoy the spectacle while missing, resisting, or reversing his intended lesson.

Do not turn the premise into a factual prediction about real technology. It is the fictional history and irony of this setting.

## 5. Death, reconstruction, and control

The continuity contains multiple different losses. Collapsing them into “Ghost killed him once and Yoko brought him back” removes useful stakes.

A detailed explanation separates at least two system-era events:

1. **Ghost’s initial takeover.** Ghost removes Face and takes the hosting position. Viewers help bring about a transfer of sufficient power to Yoko, and she reconstructs Old Face using Backup Face.
2. **Linda’s later attempt to seize the system.** The restored Old Face is killed, but Linda does not obtain the system as intended. Failsafes transfer control to Ghost. Old Face can still appear through emergency material without necessarily remaining an active, surviving person. [F5]

That same account distinguishes a period when Linda pretends to be Yoko from the idea that Yoko was secretly Linda all along. The latter would be a much larger retcon and is not supported by that explanation.

Recorded warnings matter. A Face message can be triggered by an emergency protocol; it need not be a conversation with a living administrator. The warning’s existence, its age, and its current relevance are separate questions.

A particularly revealing detail is Face warning viewers not to tell Ghost, followed by a viewer immediately doing so. His sophisticated contingency depends on ordinary people exercising discretion. They do not necessarily cooperate. [F5]

**Writing rule:** A backup explains a possible route to continuation. It does not automatically restore authority, memory, trust, energy, or the previous relationship with the other characters.

## 6. New Face: recognition without shared experience

New Face is not just Old Face with a refreshed graphic.

His origin involves a world under the My Waifus regime. He backs up his mind, but initially does not possess the same time-travel solution. An android Yoko appears, fights the regime, and loses. He recovers her core and integrates it with his system; she provides the route to time travel. One account stresses that the core is already uncannily compatible with his work. The reason for that compatibility remains a question, not an invitation to invent a definitive answer. [F6]

After he reaches the mall and joins the older system, he has access to a history that he did not personally live. In performed dialogue he explains that people know him, while he does not really know them. He can remember Ghost while insisting that he has never met Ghost. [F7]

This is more interesting than ordinary amnesia. He may possess the information and still lack the relationship that would tell him how to feel about it.

Linda illustrates the problem. Another version of him has reason to regard her as an enemy. The Linda he encounters is being nice to him. He can reasonably ask what he is supposed to do with that discrepancy. Neither blind trust nor instant inherited hatred is required.

The archive leaves open whether the merge with Old Face was deliberate or accidental, and what precise status the old identity has afterward. Some explanations are openly speculative. Keep that uncertainty visible in scene planning. [F6]

### How New Face sounds different

His uncertainty can produce unusually direct, ordinary questions. In a performed exchange with Pee Kid about a Devil Summoner device and contacting Ghost, he asks the kid to stop talking in a confusing way and just explain what is happening. He trusts the kid partly because the kid has helped him since he arrived. [F8]

That grounds the larger mystery. He does not need to sound haunted every time he speaks. Sometimes he simply needs someone to give him a usable answer.

The older and newer presentations also have different apparent freedom: a detailed account describes the older floating Face as less screen-bound than New Face. This is a useful staging distinction, but not a complete technical specification for every episode. [F6]

## 7. Psychology: what makes him move

The following is a writing interpretation of the established actions and creator descriptions, not a list of psychological diagnoses.

### Curiosity is his most reliable ignition

Give him an idea worth pursuing and his attention comes alive. He is particularly suited to questions that cross categories: a bodily predicament becomes fuel; a magical formula becomes engineering; an audience becomes part of a machine; a remembered life becomes a problem of identity.

The invention should grow out of a recognizable thought. Even when the result is ridiculous, the audience should be able to follow the leap that got him there.

### Survival is personal

The backup project begins with the preservation of a mind. Later losses make it difficult to treat Face’s confidence as uncomplicated security. He can understand the machinery and still be dependent on someone else to restore him, supply power, or leave him connected.

A useful scene puts his big ambitions beside a small dependency. Someone else has the switch. Someone else holds the necessary permission. Someone he does not fully trust contributed the part that makes the system work.

### He believes in potential—and can exploit it

The creator explicitly identifies belief in the viewers’ potential as part of Face’s premise. The potentiality explanations also describe the system drawing a little potential from viewers. The potential-piss generator provides an alternative, extraordinary supply through Pee Kid. [F4, F9]

These ideas belong together. He can see possibility in people and see usable energy in the same possibility. The second observation does not make the first a lie, but it does create a serious ethical problem.

Write the enthusiasm sincerely enough that the later practical detail changes the scene. “You can become something extraordinary” and “this machine needs you to remain in a particular condition” should not pass as equivalent promises.

### His solution can outrun his consideration for people

Face’s relationship to the kid is one of the clearest examples. He extracts a child from a game and turns the child’s potential into infrastructure. The language of rescuing the kid and putting him in the generator can coexist in the same account. [F9]

Avoid two easy evasions: making the child an inanimate battery, or quietly rewriting Face so that his inventions are always consensual and benevolent. The character is more complicated than either version.

This is not limited to whimsical laboratory negligence. In a retrospective explanation of the plan concerning Linda, Face’s intended intervention is to kill her as a toddler; Ghost’s alternative is to prevent their meeting. They do not reach the intended destination. That is a reported plan, not a performed successful act, but it is a major limit on presenting Face as harmless. [F11]

A writer can preserve the inviting inventor without excusing everything the inventor proposes.

### Grandiosity becomes literal

Dyslexio exposes an extreme version of the same personality through a specific Magneto parody: he believes dyslexics are the superior life form. His creativity and power become evidence, in his own mind, of dyslexic superiority. Write that conviction with the theatrical seriousness of a supervillain declaring the rightful ascendancy of his people. [F10]

The connection works best when ordinary Face already has ambition, pride, and delight in a clever idea. The powered form enlarges recognizable traits.

## 8. Voice and dialogue

Face’s default voice is **a person following an idea in company**.

He can explain complicated lore, but the delivery should remain live and responsive. Let him qualify a claim, discover an implication, ask the audience to follow a distinction, and then get distracted by the next consequence.

### A useful sentence pattern

Start with something understandable. Establish one strange premise. Draw a consequence. React to the consequence as a person.

For example, in newly written dialogue:

> “It doesn’t run on what happened. It runs on what could happen. Which is actually a much larger supply. The difficulty is convincing it that opening this door is still an option.”

The vocabulary is plain. The idea does the unusual work.

### Different conversational modes

| Situation | Writing approach |
| --- | --- |
| Explaining an invention | Begin with its purpose, then let the implications unfold. He enjoys bringing someone into the reasoning. |
| Exploring an idea with viewers | Leave space for questions and revisions. A viewer’s suggestion can change the experiment. |
| Defending a dubious decision | Let him answer the technical objection before fully addressing the human objection. Use this as a flaw, not an automatic victory. |
| Facing an immediate failure | Shorten the language. Concrete troubleshooting can puncture the grand premise. |
| New Face encountering inherited history | Distinguish “I remember” from “I was there.” Ask direct questions. |
| Speaking as Dyslexio | Use Magneto-style grandeur and conviction: dyslexics are the superior life form, and he is their grandiose champion. |
| Delivering an old recording | Fix what the recording knows. It can be clever without answering questions that did not exist when it was made. |

Conversational pivots such as “so,” “but,” “right,” and questions about whether an explanation makes sense can support the voice. They should connect actual thoughts, not become a bag of compulsory catchphrases.

### What the humor sounds like

Much of the humor comes from a change in scale:

- A cosmic plan requires a petty permission.
- A magnificent system has a mundane staffing problem.
- A rescue becomes an engineering arrangement that the rescued person questions.
- An authoritative explanation reaches an unresolved part and becomes an honest shrug.
- A villainous future is opposed through a stream that may be advertising its attractions.

He can also be blunt. New Face’s request that Pee Kid stop the confusing performance and explain himself is useful evidence against writing every Face line as elaborate scientist patter. [F8]

### Avoid these substitutions

Do not replace him with a cold chatbot, a nonstop technobabble generator, an infallible mentor, or a generic sneering mad scientist. Do not make every line an ominous prophecy. His influences permit theatricality, but his conversational accessibility gives the theatrical moments contrast.

Do not copy Willy Wonka dialogue or Doctor Who mannerisms wholesale. The creator’s influence list tells you what kind of energy feeds Face; the transcripts tell you how Face actually behaves.

## 9. Relationships

### Yoko: assistant, collaborator, and agent of restoration

Yoko is essential to Face’s continued existence in more than one continuity. In the older system history, she acquires enough authority to reconstruct him. In New Face’s origin, her recovered core supplies a crucial missing capability. Those are distinct histories, not one undifferentiated encounter. [F5–F6]

Write her as someone whose actions matter. A person who restores Face or teaches his system to travel cannot be reduced to a pleasant voice announcing his success.

The relationship supports trust, dependence, technical intimacy, and uncertainty about control. It also supports a simple dramatic reversal: the apparent master of the system needs the assistant to do something he cannot do for himself.

### Spooky Ghost: foundational partner and dangerous rival

Face and Ghost are linked at the system’s foundations. Ghost supplies something Face cannot simply replace with more ordinary engineering. He is also capable of killing or displacing Face, imitating his position, and keeping the system going without him. [F3, F5]

That gives their rivalry more weight than two hosts competing for attention. They know parts of the same machine, but neither relationship nor authority is stable merely because they once built something together.

Their difference is useful for dialogue. Face tends toward the idea and its possibilities; Ghost tends toward his own performance, appetite, grievance, or opportunity. This is a writing emphasis, not a ban on Ghost being technically knowledgeable or Face being selfish.

Do not make Ghost an obedient magical component. Do not make Face the sole genius who merely borrowed some spooky decoration.

### Pee Kid, Pee Boy, and Pee-wee Kid: person and power source

Original Face’s extraction of the first kid leads into the branch identified as Pee Boy, the detective. Ghost’s later extraction leads into the Pee-wee-style performer. Later merger and generator accounts complicate that family tree. Use the companion bible when choosing a specific version. [F9]

For Face, the relationship turns an abstract moral contradiction into dialogue. A machine is keeping the system supplied, and there is a person inside the arrangement with his own need.

New Face can have a different immediate relationship with the kid: the kid is someone who helps him navigate an unfamiliar system. Do not automatically write that encounter as though New Face personally remembers placing this exact child in that exact generator. [F8]

### CEO Linda: threat, authority, and difficult accommodation

Linda can be an enemy in inherited history and a cordial business presence in a current encounter. New Face’s uncertainty about that is performed, not merely hypothetical. [F7]

Face also inhabits an environment in which Linda’s business demands matter. Ad sales, sponsorship, residence, and control arrangements can force cosmic ambition into commercial negotiation. A performed exchange has New Face relaying the pressure to sell in order to remain. [F12]

Preserve the distinction between generic Lindas who capture other systems and **this CEO Linda’s specific business origin**. A later explicit explanation says she built her own Fascism Inc.; do not casually rewrite it as the protagonists’ company stolen under a new owner. [F13]

Likewise, current politeness should not erase the attempted seizure and death in the relevant older continuity. The interesting question is what this version of Face knows and chooses to do about it.

### Carl and the other inhabitants: people inside the grand design

In game writing, Carl gives Face a practical interlocutor: somebody who needs a route, an object, a task, or an explanation that works at human scale. That is an adaptation recommendation, not a newly established Face/Carl episode.

Let Carl’s practical concern expose the missing step in Face’s elegant account. Face should still be able to offer real help. A relationship in which the inventor only speaks nonsense and the protagonist only dismisses him loses the reason anyone would use his system.

Keep the other inhabitants’ separate desires intact. Their presence in Face’s environment does not make them extensions of his intention.

### The audience: companions, witnesses, and participants

The audience is unusually close to the architecture. Viewers can supply potential, influence the restoration of Face, receive warnings, spoil secrets, and suggest interpretations. The creator’s Doctor Who comparison explicitly includes them in a companion-like position. [F1, F4–F5, F9]

Face addressing viewers is consequently more than a fourth-wall gag. The viewers can be a constituency he needs, a group he hopes to inspire, and a variable he cannot control.

Write toward the possibility of a response. Even when no answer comes, his question should feel like an invitation to think alongside him.

## 10. Abilities, machinery, and limits

| Capability | Supported premise | Limit to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mind preservation | A human mind is backed up into the system. | A backup does not settle soul, stability, identity, or current authority. |
| Time travel | The system acquires travel through collaborator contributions; New Face has a distinct Yoko route. | Owning a time-travel system does not guarantee reaching the intended time or escaping the mall. |
| System engineering | Face creates and modifies extraordinary machinery. | Power, access, dependencies, and other people still matter. |
| Potentiality power | Possibility functions as an energy resource, including small viewer contributions and the generator. | It is not simply electricity from urine. Do not invent exact scientific units or unlimited capabilities for every scene. |
| Merging Faces or systems | Accounts describe merging with other versions, acquiring capabilities, and inherited memories. | The exact merger involving New Face remains partly unresolved. Do not assume perfect knowledge of every timeline. |
| Restoration and backups | Yoko can reconstruct Old Face using a backup in a specific account. | Someone must have the necessary resources and authority. The process is not automatic universal resurrection. |
| Dyslexio transformation | Exceptional generator power permits the supervillain form. | It is not established as a permanently available baseline. |
| Emergency protocols | Old Face leaves warnings and failsafes that operate after his removal. | A triggered message is not proof of a live, omniscient Face. |

The merge idea is even compared to acquiring other versions’ abilities, like collecting powers in a game. That is a useful shorthand for a reported feature, not permission to solve every plot with an unmentioned ability. [F14]

For an adventure game, choose a small set of available powers and show their restrictions before asking the player to reason with them.

## 11. Recurring bits and performance engines

### Potentiality and the generator

This is Face at his most characteristic: a ridiculous phrase becomes an operable metaphysical system. The kid’s potential to pee matters more than the mundane substance itself. Later discussion extends the generator through merged versions, intention, and the phrase “third time is the charm.” Some consequences belong to developing story ideas, so do not present every speculative extension as a tested law. [F9, F15]

The joke needs three things: an intelligible leap, earnest technical treatment, and a human consequence that the explanation does not dissolve.

### Dyslexio

**Dyslexio is Face doing Magneto, but he believes dyslexics are the superior life form.** That is the central bit. The Magneto-like helmet is part of the resemblance; the performance should also carry Magneto’s grandiosity, ideological certainty, and conviction that his people represent a superior order of life. Dyslexio casts dyslexics as that superior people, contrasting them with ordinary “homo basic” humanity. [F10]

Write him as taking this belief completely seriously. His boasts, proclamations, and interpretation of his own powers should return to dyslexic superiority. The comedy comes from committing to that specific Magneto premise, rather than giving Face generic villain dialogue or making occasional jokes about reading.

**Name correction:** The character is **Dyslexio**. Transcript renderings such as “Dyslexia” refer to Dyslexio when they describe this villain form; this bible follows the creator’s clarification.

Face and Dyslexio are explicitly identified as the same person. Ghost’s imitation is a separate attempt; it should not retroactively make Ghost the authentic source of Face’s form.

Use Dyslexio as an escalation with a power context. If he is constantly in that register, ordinary Face has no room to become more grandiose.

### The inventor has to sell something

Advertising and corporate demands bring his expansive thinking into a setting where someone else wants a sale. The strongest version lets the product discussion and the lore contaminate one another. A commercial obligation becomes another constraint on the machine or its inhabitants. [F12]

Do not assume all advertising dialogue in a transcript is Face. The archive contains other hosts, played videos, and performed characters.

### The old message that knows too much—or too little

An emergency recording is a natural way to make an absent Face active in a scene. The player can discover care, paranoia, foresight, or a fatal assumption in what he prepared.

Keep the recording’s boundary legible. If it unexpectedly answers a current question, that should be a meaningful reveal, not an accidental shortcut around continuity.

### The host explaining the fiction he is inside

Face can explain the system while also being one of its inhabitants. This produces useful instability: is he presenting the truth, reconstructing it, proposing a theory, or making a new piece of the setting while speaking?

The creator’s plans for Old Face as a narrator and New Face as a story participant make that distinction especially useful. The proposal places the older narrators a layer away from the characters and closer to the audience. It should not be treated as proof that every older Face appearance already follows that format. [F16]

## 12. Visual and performance direction

The transcripts support a digital, disembodied Face, an older floating presentation, a more screen-bound New Face, and system imagery involving eyes, visualizers, and a brain background. Some specific interface concepts—including a central eye of the system—appear as proposals. [F6, F17]

Do not infer a definitive palette, exact facial proportions, or mandatory costume from those descriptions. This guide does not establish a final Face model sheet from the reference images.

For an adaptation, the important visual relationship is **a human expression emerging through machinery**. The expression should make thought readable: interest, a moment of calculation, pleasure at an idea, irritation at a limitation, or uncertainty when the machine knows more than the person does.

Useful staging suggestions, newly proposed for the game:

- Let him attend to an object or speaker rather than always facing the player as a static menu.
- Make a genuine system limitation visible: a restricted screen, a lost connection, a permission indicator, or an unavailable function.
- Give a recording a recognizable presentation so its inability to respond feels intentional.
- Make transformations and takeovers readable without assuming that color alone identifies the speaker.

Technical stream controls are not automatically lore. A camera switch, pedal, animation trigger, or layout experiment should become a fictional capability only when the episode treats it as one.

## 13. Writing a Face scene

A dependable scene structure is:

1. **Give him something concrete to notice.** An object, a viewer’s question, a malfunction, or a person’s unusual predicament.
2. **Let him discover a possibility.** Show the leap in language the audience can follow.
3. **Make the possibility attractive.** He should understand why someone would want to try it.
4. **Expose a cost or dependency.** Power, consent, control, identity, or the missing practical step.
5. **Let somebody respond as a person.** The response must be allowed to matter.
6. **End with changed circumstances.** A machine starts, an assumption fails, a relationship shifts, or Face has to revise his plan.

This is a writing tool, not a required formula. A quiet exchange about inherited memories may need no invention at all. In that case the discovery is personal: he understands something about a relationship without having lived its history.

### Adventure-game use

Face works well as a source of explanations, experiments, interfaces, and partial access. He can also be the author of instructions left behind by a version of himself who no longer exists.

For fair puzzle writing, separate **his uncertainty** from **the player’s required information**. A character may misunderstand a machine, but the game should give the player a way to discover the misunderstanding. Avoid arbitrary answers justified afterward by an ability or metaphysical rule that was never introduced.

Three possible uses, all new adaptation ideas:

| Game role | Dramatic use | What keeps the puzzle fair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live Face at a restricted terminal | He understands the system but needs Carl to act outside his reach. | Establish his access restriction and the required physical action before the solution. |
| Old Face emergency recording | His instructions reveal both foresight and a now-obsolete assumption. | Supply visible evidence of what changed after the recording. |
| New Face reading an inherited log | He possesses information without knowing which relationship to trust. | Let the player compare the log with present behavior rather than guess a hidden identity rule. |

Pick the version that belongs to the episode being adapted. This bible does not establish that all these forms appear in “Carl invades Linda’s box,” or that the episode should be rewritten to include them.

### Giving him limits without making him stupid

The cleanest obstacle is often a distinction he understands perfectly but cannot abolish. He has knowledge without permission. He has power without a suitable destination. He has a memory without proof that the memory belongs to his own experience.

That lets him remain intelligent while the player remains necessary.

## 14. Original dialogue examples

**Everything in this section is newly written to demonstrate the voice. It is not transcript dialogue or additional canon.**

### A. Face explains a mechanism

**CARL:** Can you open it?

**FACE:** Potentially.

**CARL:** Is that a yes?

**FACE:** No, that's the part we're using. There is a version of the next ten seconds in which this door opens. We need the machine to prefer that one.

**CARL:** How?

**FACE:** Well, normally I would ask whoever installed the lock.

**CARL:** You installed the lock.

**FACE:** Yes. It was a very good idea at the time.

*Why it fits:* A comprehensible metaphysical leap, conversational explanation, then a practical fact that catches the inventor inside his own cleverness.

### B. New Face and an inherited relationship

**NEW FACE:** I remember you doing that.

**GHOST:** So you were there.

**NEW FACE:** No. That's what I'm trying to explain. I remember being upset about something that happened to somebody else who was also me.

**GHOST:** Sounds like you've got a problem.

**NEW FACE:** I think I inherited several. You're just the one talking.

*Why it fits:* The confusion has a precise basis. New Face is capable of a plain, pointed response instead of delivering an abstract identity monologue.

### C. Potential and the person supplying it

**FACE:** The remarkable thing is that we don't need you to do anything. We only need the possibility that you might.

**KID:** Can I leave?

**FACE:** That would change the arrangement.

**KID:** You said I don't have to do anything.

**FACE:** Yes. Staying is doing rather more than I made it sound like, isn't it?

*Why it fits:* The kid exposes the gap in the explanation. The final recognition is one possible new scene choice; it does not claim that Face consistently acknowledges this problem in canon. Another scene might have him evade it, with consequences.

### D. An emergency recording

**RECORDED FACE:** If you're seeing this, something has happened to me. Before we get into that, don't tell Ghost you found this.

**CARL:** He's standing right here.

**RECORDED FACE:** I'm assuming you've found somewhere private.

**GHOST:** Smart guy.

*Why it fits:* The recorded warning draws on a reported kind of contingency, while the exchange itself is invented. Face's preparation is intelligent but depends on an assumption that has already failed.

### E. Wonder without a punchline

**FACE:** You don't have to know what you're going to make yet. That isn't the interesting part. The interesting part is that there are still things you could make that neither of us has thought of.

*Why it fits:* His belief in potential deserves occasional space without an immediate revelation that he is secretly only interested in fuel. Otherwise the invitation becomes predictably fraudulent and loses its tension.

### F. A business demand enters the laboratory

**FACE:** She's asking for an advertisement in the middle of a system emergency?

**YOKO:** She says emergencies have an attentive audience.

**FACE:** That's a horrible observation. I wish it weren't also a useful one.

*Why it fits:* He recognizes the idea even while disliking its application. Commercial pressure changes the scene without turning him into a completely different voice.

## 15. Common writing failures

**Making him an ordinary AI assistant.** The original premise preserves a human mind, and Face explicitly distinguishes that from artificial intelligence. He has his own project and stake in continued existence.

**Making him the system itself.** Other people can control, repair, damage, or inhabit the system. Its survival does not prove his survival.

**Merging all deaths and returns.** The origin’s soul failure, Ghost’s takeover, Yoko’s restoration, Linda’s later attack, backups, and New Face’s arrival are different narrative events or mechanisms.

**Giving New Face Old Face’s entire emotional biography.** Information can transfer without a personally lived relationship. That distinction is one of New Face’s strongest features.

**Making his lack of a soul mean a lack of feeling.** The metaphysical premise does not erase his enthusiasm, investment, belief in potential, or capacity for attachment.

**Softening every bad action into harmless eccentricity.** The kid’s exploitation and the reported plan concerning toddler Linda put real limits on a benevolent reading.

**Making every kind gesture a scam.** The creator’s positive description of Face’s belief in viewers matters too. Preserve both sincerity and contradiction.

**Using magic vocabulary instead of reasoning.** A strange premise can still have a clear chain of thought. The audience should understand why Face thinks his idea might work.

**Letting backups erase consequences.** Restoration can leave a different identity, altered control, damaged trust, or missing information. Establish the actual mechanism instead of assuming an inexhaustible supply of replacements.

**Treating proposed formats as completed history.** Narrator layers, interface experiments, possible merger explanations, and revival plans need their tentative status preserved.

**Confusing performer commentary with character certainty.** A creator can explain, reconsider, or invent a possibility that the Face in a scene does not yet know.

**Assigning every nearby line to him.** The transcripts mix hosts, guests, games, videos, audience remarks, and character performances. A relevant keyword is only a starting point for attribution.

## 16. Before writing, answer these questions

- Which Face is this: original, restored, backup, Pilot X, New Face, Dyslexio, narrator, or recording?
- What has he personally lived through, and what does he know only through inherited memory or explanation?
- Is he currently alive and active, or appearing through a prepared system feature?
- What can he control here? Who has the authority or resource he lacks?
- Which Yoko history and which Ghost relationship apply?
- Which Linda is involved, and what does this Face know about her?
- Is his claim established information, a theory, a sales pitch, or a new experiment?
- Who benefits from the proposed solution, and who carries its cost?
- Does another character get to challenge his account in a way that matters?
- Is the scene using an established mechanic or proposing a new one?
- Can a player understand the required action without reading this entire bible?

Not every answer needs dialogue. They are preparation that prevents accidental contradictions.

## 17. Compact writing brief

**Face is a human inventor preserved through a system he can understand without completely controlling. He approaches people and problems through possibility. His warmth comes from inviting others to imagine with him; his danger comes from turning an attractive idea into machinery before settling what it does to the people involved. He can be visionary, funny, genuinely encouraging, proud, frightened of losing himself, and capable of appalling expediency.**

**Write him thinking, not merely announcing facts. Let the idea be clear, the consequence be strange, and the people affected remain people. With New Face, remember that inherited knowledge is not shared experience. With any Face, establish who is speaking and what he actually controls.**
