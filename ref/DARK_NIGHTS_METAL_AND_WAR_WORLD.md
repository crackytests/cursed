# DARK NIGHTS: METAL & WAR WORLD

**Setting, faction, continuity, and game-writing reference · 7 October 2026**

**Expanded archive revision · 9 November 2026**

A transcript-grounded write-up of the stream’s far-future Dark Nights: Metal setting and the interdimensional War World associated with New Face. This covers their relationship, the original three factions, later additions, and the material available for stories and games.

**The central distinction: Dark Nights: Metal is the far-future vision and faction collection. War World is the setting that brings those factions into conflict over New Face and his system.**

They become connected in the creator’s development discussions. They should not automatically be treated as two names for one already-completed, exhaustively mapped world.

The title here follows your spelling, **Dark Nights: Metal**. The transcripts also render it as Dark Knights Metal, Dark Knight’s Metal, and other variants. The creator explicitly says the name was borrowed from DC. The lore in this document is your stream’s setting; the shared title does not import DC characters or cosmology. [W1]

## 1. What kind of material this is

The archive contains several layers:

- **The art-event premise:** a far-future war among the Linda Hordes, Spooky Ones, and Endless Yoko.
- **Open mysteries:** the fate of humanity, the date, and whether the future factions descend from the familiar cast.
- **Developed faction explanations:** astral spirits inhabiting machines; nanomachine bioroids; Yoko’s expanding civilization.
- **War World development:** New Face’s independent existence attracts competing factions, and he escapes with help from recovered Yoko technology.
- **Game experiments:** card mechanics, map campaigns, salvage expeditions, and additional factions.

The creator openly describes some explanations as undecided or newly being worked out. This document preserves those statuses. A detailed later explanation adds usable material without proving that every earlier mystery was resolved identically across all versions.

**Writing and game suggestions below are interpretation or new proposals. They are not recovered scenes.**

## 2. Dark Nights: Metal — the far-future vision

The original presentation describes an extremely distant future in which war continues among three forces:

1. **The Linda Hordes**
2. **The Spooky Ones**
3. **Endless Yoko**

The setting is presented through art galleries, collectible stream cards, and an event framed as a Cracky Reeds and Linda co-production. Linda argues about billing and objects to the sound of “Linda Hordes,” which the host insists is “hordes.” That presentation joke is part of how the setting enters the stream. [W1–W2]

The creator deliberately avoids a fixed date. The description allows an end-times or possibly post-human setting and explicitly leaves open whether ordinary humans are still alive. [W2]

**Do not turn “possibly post-human” into confirmed human extinction.** The uncertainty is part of the atmosphere.

### The imaginative appeal

The familiar stream characters have become distant shapes in a future nobody completely understands. An assistant may have become an empire. A ghost may have inspired a machine-inhabiting spiritual civilization. A waifu image may persist as a self-repairing horde.

That is stronger than simply putting the current cast in futuristic armor. Time has changed the scale and possibly the meaning of what they were.

The opening presentation also treats the images as glimpses into the future rather than a fully surveyed world. The host says he has seen it but has not interviewed its inhabitants, and Ghost’s absence prevents simply traveling there to answer everything. [W2]

For writing, this supports artifacts, unreliable identifications, fragments of history, and discoveries whose significance becomes clear gradually.

## 3. The original three factions at a glance

| Faction | Strongest supported identity | What distinguishes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linda Hordes / Linda Bioroids / Lindoids | AI-controlled, self-repairing bodies using nanotechnology and available matter. | Survival through consumption, reconstruction, and reuse. |
| Spooky Ones | Initially unexplained ghostlike machines; subsequently developed as astral spirits possessing machines. | Survival of a person or spirit beyond the destruction of a particular body. |
| Endless Yoko | A far-future authority overseeing civilizations, potentially across multiple realities. | Assistance, improvement, coordination, and the power to decide who should wield dangerous capabilities. |

The contrast is useful even before individual leaders or units are invented:

**The Hordes rebuild bodies. The Spooky Ones move beyond bodies. Endless Yoko organizes what bodies and societies can become.**

That is a writing synthesis, not a literal in-world slogan.

## 4. The Linda Hordes

### Reported design and developed lore

The earliest description calls them bioroids capable of repairing themselves with almost anything: metal, human bodies, and other material. A later explanation specifies nanomachines and compares them to zombies. [W2–W4]

Later faction discussion distinguishes them from soul-bearing Lindas. They have AI, but not the souls associated with an actual Linda system. The creator briefly considers whether the horde might collectively constitute one mind, then explicitly leaves the thought unresolved. [W4]

Use that distinction carefully:

- A Linda-looking bioroid is not automatically CEO Linda.
- The bodies are not individually established as Ghost’s ex-wives.
- Their AI does not prove they are ordinary mindless zombies.
- A collective consciousness is floated, not settled.

### Behavior and threat

They make destruction unreliable. A battlefield full of wreckage may be a repair supply rather than a victory.

Their visual horror follows from an indifferent approach to material. Human remains and machine parts can become inputs to the same reconstruction process. Their resemblance to a familiar, attractive character can persist through that process.

**Writing interpretation:** They are frightening because they make bodily boundaries provisional. What used to be someone, or something useful, can become material for the next body.

### Game-development direction

Proposed mechanics include consuming things, growing through kills, sacrificing or repurposing units, and reassembling after destruction. A prototype gives reassembly a cost and limitations; it is not literally infinite resurrection. [W5]

For an adventure game, possible new encounters include:

- A disabled unit that becomes dangerous when the player restores power to nearby machinery.
- A route blocked by a body that keeps rebuilding from debris.
- A machine that can be distracted by a more accessible material supply.
- A apparently safe salvage field whose pieces are still participating in a repair process.

Those encounters are new proposals. The transcript does not provide exact detection ranges, repair rates, or universal weaknesses.

### Relationship to the familiar Linda

The event’s presenter can be Linda without every future body being her personal puppet. The art-show billing and the faction’s command structure are different questions.

Likewise, CEO Linda’s more specific history should not be overwritten by a generic statement that all Linda-like entities are identical captured systems. Refer to her own bible when she appears as a person rather than a faction motif.

## 5. The Spooky Ones

### The initial mystery

Early descriptions deliberately refuse to explain them. They are strange, ghostlike or robotic entities that might be connected to Ghost, might be Ghosts, or might be something else. The creator invites interpretation rather than giving an origin. [W1–W2]

That is the presentation layer, not the full extent of the available lore.

### The developed origin

A subsequent explanation supplies a concrete concept: Ghost, or another future version of him, teaches people astral projection. Someone who dies while projected can remain as a spirit. That spirit can possess robots and machines. The faction consists of beings surviving through that relationship between spirit and machinery. [W3–W4]

This is repeated in another card-game discussion, making it more than an isolated visual guess.

The uncertainty retained in the fuller explanation is **which Ghost founded or taught them**, not whether the creator ever developed an explanation at all.

### Why they want New Face

In the War World pitch, New Face matters to them because he is connected to their original leader: he is another version of the person associated with Ghost. [W3]

This supports recognition and competing expectations. It does not establish that New Face is their reincarnated god, that they obey him, or that they can prove every detail of his identity.

### Writing them

**Interpretation:** Their machine bodies need not tell the whole truth about their personalities. An intimidating shell could contain an ordinary person’s habits, memories, or unfinished commitments.

Let them differ from the Hordes in what survives. Destroying a bioroid may interrupt a material process; destroying a Spooky One’s shell may leave a spirit looking for another machine. The exact rules for transfer, memory, and final death still need to be established for a particular story.

### Game proposals

- Follow one spirit through several machines by a repeated mannerism or distinctive knowledge.
- Negotiate for passage with someone who recognizes Face but mistakes the version.
- Recover a suitable shell instead of treating repair as ordinary mechanical maintenance.
- Disable a machine without assuming the inhabitant has been killed.

Card discussions also propose avoiding destruction or death as a faction emphasis. Treat those mechanics as expressions of the premise, not a complete metaphysical law. [W5]

## 6. Endless Yoko

### Scale matters

A particularly important clarification distinguishes **Empress/Evil Yoko** from **Endless Yoko**.

The familiar original Yoko sent to the Mandolin reality has helped one Earth fulfill its potential and become its ruler. Endless Yoko operates at a much greater scale: multiple realities and spacefaring civilizations under her authority. [W6]

The creator leaves open whether she is that Yoko much later, another Yoko, or an equivalent branch. Other tellings identify her more directly with the Mandolin story. Preserve those differences instead of treating the lineage as uniformly settled.

The present-day Empress is not automatically already Endless Yoko. One explanation explicitly wants that separation so interactions with the current character still matter and her future can remain changeable. [W6]

### Her governing idea

The far-future presentation calls her the graceful authority of spacetime. Later development connects her success to giving people assistance that lets them realize their potential. [W1, W3]

Her civilization should not be reduced to an army of identical Yoko portraits. The creator explicitly allows other units under her control or umbrella. [W7]

### Her objection to Face

In the developed War World pitch, she does not trust Face with interdimensional power. She may want to stop him acquiring that freedom because he would become another problem across realities. One telling ties that distrust to the magic-trick history; another emphasizes her assessment that the power is too much for him. [W3]

That gives her a motive beyond conquest for its own sake. She can believe she is preventing an irresponsible actor from creating a much larger crisis.

**Writing interpretation:** Her most effective threat can be a persuasive offer of help that also removes the right to refuse her supervision.

Do not settle her entire moral status by the nickname “Evil Yoko.” The transcripts credit the original with helping a world. They do not provide enough information to certify every future policy as benevolent or oppressive.

### Gameplay direction

The card-design material associates her with buffs, upgrades, economy, and later the label **Optimizers**. [W5, W8]

For adventure writing, that suggests coordinated infrastructure and systems that become much more effective when linked. A proposed encounter might involve restoring one local service while discovering that the restoration also brings the area under imperial oversight.

That is new design. The source does not establish a compulsory empire-wide hive mind or universal remote control over every machine.

## 7. War World — why everybody converges

War World is described as New Face’s home setting and an interdimensional conflict zone. The creator explicitly identifies it as a place to bring the Dark Nights: Metal factions together with other factions developed through the stream. [W3, W9]

The central prize is **New Face’s system—and therefore New Face himself**.

An independent Face is rare. Earlier searches mostly found Lindas, broken systems, and authoritarian machine governments. A surviving, uncaptured system becomes an anomaly other powers notice. [W3]

A separate explanation makes the scarcity especially clear: there are many captured or Linda-like outcomes, while a free lone operator is unusual. The factions fight to obtain him. [W9]

### The factions do not all want the same outcome

| Faction | Motive supplied or suggested in the development account |
| --- | --- |
| Linda forces | Capture or exploit the system; the broader lore already establishes systems being appropriated. |
| Endless Yoko | Prevent or control Face’s access to interdimensional travel; she distrusts his handling of power. |
| Spooky Ones | His connection to their founding leader makes him important. |
| Other possible entrants | Capture, destroy, claim, or otherwise use the anomaly, depending on the faction. |

The exact command structures, alliances, territory boundaries, and campaigns are not supplied. Those remain writing work.

The strongest dramatic consequence is that **Face is not simply a soldier choosing a side. He is a person being treated as strategic infrastructure.**

A faction can sincerely believe it is saving him while also denying that he should remain independent.

## 8. New Face’s escape story

The detailed origin account gives a usable sequence:

1. New Face exists in the war-torn world as a backed-up mind and sought-after system.
2. He is hiding while different forces try to obtain him.
3. A Yoko android appears and fights in that world.
4. She is defeated.
5. He recovers her body or core and integrates or reverse-engineers it.
6. Her architecture is already compatible, and she supplies the knowledge needed for time travel.
7. He escapes and eventually reaches the Mall of the Future. [W9–W10]

The compatibility is a mystery within the explanation. It should not be answered casually with a new definitive backstory.

The recovered Yoko’s connection to Evil Yoko is discussed at different levels of certainty elsewhere. For this setting, the firm useful element is the intervention, defeat, recovery, compatibility, and escape; a specific identity reveal needs a deliberate continuity choice.

The original Face/Ghost partnership is a different origin. Do not replace New Face’s Yoko contribution with Ghost’s original ritual simply because both produce time travel.

### New Face in rehearsal and in game development

While rehearsing New Face aloud, the creator has him place his own world in War World and say an Evil Yoko android came there to defend him. New Face says he reverse engineered her after she was destroyed. A separate game-development discussion names Dai Yoko as the one who tries to save New Face in that game's War World. Keep each name attached to its telling until a source or an explicit adaptation decision connects them. ## 9. Time, invasion, and the mall

One development discussion considers a rule: travel into a timeline becomes possible only as far back as the invention of time travel in that timeline. Inventing it opens the world to outside forces. [W6]

This is an explicitly proposed rule, attributed by the creator to an influence. It is not securely established as the law governing every time-travel scene in the stream.

It also creates a sequencing question worth resolving before writing a game: **what first makes the world accessible, if Face is already hiding from factions before recovering Yoko’s travel capability?** The sources do not settle that into one clean order. Another inventor, an earlier opening, or a distinction between local and personal travel could solve it in an adaptation, but each would be a new decision.

The same discussion proposes that the factions lose track of New Face because the mall lies outside ordinary time and space. Getting caught there accidentally protects him from pursuers who did not expect it. [W6]

This gives the mall a double function: trap and refuge. Remaining there can be rational even when its owner is difficult or dangerous.

Do not turn that into guaranteed permanent invulnerability. The explanation is developing, and the wider story includes ways of locating or threatening the system.

## 10. Tycoon X and later factions

The original collection has three factions. Later game development adds **Tycoon X / Industrialist X**, with Businessman X also considered as a name. Do not retroactively list him as one of the original three. [W8, W11]

He is associated with a Face-like faction, red or Char-like glasses, industry, vehicles, and siege. His identity is deliberately unresolved: he might be connected to the familiar cast or represent a future outcome, but the creator says that question need not be answered yet. [W11]

A discussion also entertains a rivalry in which Tycoon X and Endless Yoko compete across realities over who can lead worlds toward a better outcome. That is a story possibility, not an established history of their war. [W11]

Later Pilot X material and the Crack Zinger are mentioned as possible expansion content. They are not proof that every Tycoon X unit is already a super robot.

Other possibilities include an evil-Face faction, a Carl-related faction or neutral location, and additional stream concepts. These demonstrate an expandable setting; they do not establish a finished roster. [W3, W5, W12]

## 11. The alternate-Earth salvage concept

A separate game discussion offers a particularly strong use for Endless Yoko’s scale: explore the multiverse sideways rather than only traveling outward through space. Other Earths are already potentially suitable worlds, even when they have suffered different catastrophes. [W13]

The proposed mission is to enter another Earth, locate its version of the stream’s system, retrieve or salvage it, and get out. Endless Yoko is considered as the employer. The journey could involve a small party and an Oregon Trail-like expedition structure, with the destination’s condition varying by reality.

A local Yoko might still defend the system. Some worlds could retain civilization in a strange form rather than being uniformly dead.

This remains a game pitch, but it develops War World’s central resource into a playable mission: **the object everyone wants may contain someone who does not want to be retrieved.**

That last formulation is a writing interpretation. It creates room for rescue, theft, negotiation, and conflicting definitions of recovery without inventing an entirely separate setting.

## 12. Tone, imagery, and ordinary life

Dark Nights: Metal begins with images of an extreme future, but a story needs more than imposing factions.

Useful supported visual foundations include:

- Linda-like bodies with damaged, undead, necromancer-like, and self-repairing qualities.
- Ghost-associated machinery whose inhabitants may be spiritual rather than artificial.
- Yoko as an imperial or cosmic authority, with civilization-scale reach.
- Familiar stream identities surviving in altered or uncertain forms.

The archive contains names for particular pieces and cards, but a striking title does not automatically establish a named historical ruler or a complete unit class.

**Writing recommendation:** Keep the distant-future imagery large and the immediate problems concrete. A spirit needs a functioning chassis. A convoy cannot cross a field of reusable wreckage. An imperial official offers restoration in exchange for authority. Face needs a way out before someone else decides what he is for.

These examples are new scene ideas. They turn the faction premises into actions without requiring an encyclopedia before play begins.

Humor can remain recognizably part of the stream. Linda can care about the wording of her faction name. Ghost’s immense spiritual legacy can coexist with his familiar appetite for credit. Face can understand the machine and still be unable to get anyone to regard him as a person instead of equipment.

Do not make every future inhabitant merely the current host in a different costume. The scale becomes convincing when descendants, followers, machines, and institutions develop interests of their own.

## 13. Game and story possibilities

All concepts here are adaptation suggestions, not completed transcript events.

| Concept | Player’s immediate objective | What it explores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New Face’s escape** | Keep the system independent, recover Yoko, obtain a route out. | Being pursued by factions that value your capabilities more than your wishes. |
| **Salvage for Endless Yoko** | Recover a system from an alternate Earth. | Whether retrieval means rescue, acquisition, or erasure of local autonomy. |
| **A Spooky One without a shell** | Find a suitable machine while carrying knowledge somebody wants. | Identity and survival independent of a particular body. |
| **Across a Linda salvage field** | Reach a destination without supplying the Hordes with what they need. | Reconstruction as an environmental threat rather than endless combat. |
| **A diplomatic claim on Face** | Evaluate competing offers of protection. | Different factions sincerely believing they should control the same person. |
| **A future that may not become ours** | Discover evidence linking familiar characters to future rulers. | The difference between a possible outcome and a fixed destiny. |

For an adventure game, the strongest starting concept is **New Face’s escape**. It has an immediate protagonist, a contested location, distinct pursuers, a consequential ally, and an endpoint already connected to the stream.

The salvage expedition is the stronger structure for repeated adventures. Each Earth can have a compact mystery and a different version of the system without requiring the player to resolve the entire multiversal war.

Neither concept requires simulating every faction’s economy. The strategic setting can exist around a small number of playable rooms, people, and decisions.

## 14. Suggested dramatic structure for a War World game

**New proposal: a contained escape story.**

**Opening:** New Face is keeping his system running in concealment. The player learns what he can control and what he cannot physically reach. A faction’s approach makes remaining hidden untenable.

**First expansion:** Encounters establish different claims on him. The Hordes threaten material capture, the Spooky Ones recognize a connection he does not fully understand, and Yoko’s larger authority has reasons to restrict him.

**Turning point:** The intervening Yoko android loses. Her recovery becomes an urgent practical task and an identity mystery. Why is she already compatible with him?

**Final movement:** The player assembles a working escape route using the recovered knowledge. Leaving requires preventing pursuit or capture long enough to activate it; it need not require defeating three civilizations.

**Ending:** Escape succeeds, but the destination is the mall. The player gains freedom from the immediate war and acquires a new set of dependencies.

The sources support the underlying escape sequence. Exact encounters, locations, puzzle solutions, and the order of faction contacts would be new screenplay work.

Avoid a climax that casually confirms every mystery. Leaving with a functioning relationship and an unanswered question about Yoko can be a complete ending.

## 16. Compact setting brief

**Dark Nights: Metal is a distant future in which familiar stream identities have become factions: self-repairing Linda bodies, spirits inhabiting machines, and Yoko’s expanding authority across civilizations. Its earliest form deliberately leaves history and survival uncertain. Later explanations give the factions specific origins and motives.**

**War World brings those forces into a shared conflict around New Face, a rare system that still belongs to itself. Some want to capture him, some want to control his access to other realities, and some recognize a connection to their own origins. His escape through recovered Yoko technology leads toward the mall, while the war remains a setting capable of generating many other stories.**
