# Dyslexio Out of Order Game Design

**PRETEND CO. · SUPER-16 · Design proposal · 12 October 2026**

**Expanded archive revision · 9 November 2026**

**DYSLEXIO: OUT OF ORDER** is a puzzle adventure in the Mall of the Future. Play as Face's powered supervillain form, move physical words between electronic signs, and use the resulting changes to cross doors, camera zones, guard posts, and conveyors. Every useful rearrangement becomes proof, in Dyslexio's mind, that dyslexics should rule the world. Linda sees a commercially useful improvement to her mall.

Build six complete, authored rooms first. They must establish a readable rule system, unlimited undo, restart, a short story, and an ending. Expand within four mall departments only after those rooms work in playtests. The initial version has no combat, time limit, procedural puzzles, or unrestricted text input.

This document develops the proposal in [Suggest a new game](codex://threads/01a133b2-b481-70a2-903d-afddb272831e). Source lore, existing game adaptations, and new decisions for this cartridge are distinguished below. The mechanics, rooms, machine, dialogue, and endings described here are proposed game content.

## Character and continuity foundation

The transcript archive establishes a more specific character than a villain who makes spelling mistakes. Dyslexio is Face adopting a Magneto-style supervillain identity. His claim is that dyslexics are the superior life form, with ordinary humanity dismissed as “homo basic.” He commits to that claim with theatrical seriousness. His stated power is creativity; the helmet supplies the visual reference. [T1]

Face and Dyslexio are explicitly the same person. Ghost has attempted an imitation, which the retrospective accounts distinguish from the authentic form. This game stars Face's Dyslexio, with no playable Ghost substitute. [T2, T3]

The transformation needs exceptional power. A retrospective connects it to the potentiality generator and describes the available power as effectively unlimited. Potentiality is a fictional resource, distinguished in the account from ordinary urine used as fuel. Dyslexio should therefore remain a special powered episode, rather than Face's everyday state. [T3]

The existing FACE game turns that premise into magnetic shots that bend bullets and rearrange enemy words. Moving words between signs is a further game adaptation of that mechanic. The transcripts do not establish a universal ability to rewrite every sentence into reality. [G1]

Linda built the Mall of the Future, which exists outside time and space. Accounts also describe a damaged mall and limits to its systems. The cartridge uses one locally operating department installation; it does not establish that the whole mall has been repaired. [T4, T5]

## Premise and player experience

Linda installs the **POSTED POLICY SYSTEM**, which connects approved signs to the mall's doors, surveillance, security posts, and delivery belts. She advertises it as a way to eliminate uncertainty: the property obeys the posted policy.

Face examines a connection to the existing potentiality supply. With enough power available, he puts on the helmet and declares the installation evidence of the coming dyslexic ascendancy. He takes control of its movable word plates. Linda wants him to reach the certification terminal and demonstrate a saleable system. He intends to arrive as its conqueror.

This power connection and incident are original connective fiction. Keep their introduction brief. The game does not reenact the generator's origin, resolve the different child branches, or place the cartridge at an exact point in the conflicting timelines.

The pleasure of play is seeing both sides of a clever change. Opening a door can activate a camera because the same **NOT** has left another sign. A useful word stays useful after its first job; the player learns when it can be borrowed safely.

Three design requirements govern every room:

- The player can predict the effect of a sentence from its visible words and target.
- Consequences appear on the board, including the disadvantage caused at the source sign.
- Experimentation is cheap: mistakes can be undone without losing progress or sitting through a speech.

## Story and cast

**Dyslexio** is the player. He floats along the floor grid, with a cape, helmet, projected face, and wireframe hands. His reach across the room expresses creative control, but walls and security still constrain his movement. A door opening two tiles away deserves the performance of a historical revolution.

**CEO Linda** owns the system. She interrupts at department transitions and the conclusion, rather than after every move. She can recognize a clever result while insisting that it occurred on her property. Her competence and authority should survive the joke. Follow [her character guide](J:/carladv/CEO_LINDA_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md:13).

**The Clerk** explains the first sign and inventories the word plates. The explanation concerns slots and machine behavior. It does not explain the whole cosmology.

**The Spellchecker** is the final room's security attendant, reusing the existing FACE antagonist as a puzzle opponent. It enforces displayed rules. It never secretly changes grammar, steals a word, or overrides undo. Its procedural stubbornness supplies the opposition without a combat system. Its role here is new cartridge fiction. [G2]

Carl, Ghost, Yoko, and the kids do not need new playable roles. The core cast is sufficient for this story.

### Story sequence

1. **Installation.** A brief ordinary Face and Linda exchange establishes the machine and the unusual power connection. The helmet transformation leads into the title.
2. **Entrance.** The Clerk shows one locked door and one spare word. Dyslexio declares his first victory.
3. **Security and deliveries.** Borrowed words create consequences. Linda begins discussing a product demonstration while Dyslexio imagines the hierarchy of his future society.
4. **Certification.** The Spellchecker guards the final route. The player solves a room using the same rules learned earlier.
5. **Disposition.** At the terminal, Linda offers to certify the installation. The player chooses between leaving the word-control connection operating and disconnecting it.

### Endings

**CERTIFIED SUPERIOR.** Leave the connection operating. Dyslexio declares victory. Linda registers the demonstration as a service on her property and prints a maintenance assignment bearing his name. He has conquered a door and acquired a job. The board solution remains a real achievement; the ending punctures his interpretation of it.

**OUT OF ORDER.** Disconnect the installation's word-control connection after reaching the terminal. The room-clear transition first moves Face beyond the controlled floor; the installation then returns its objects to their displayed factory states, and Face loses access to this episode's sustaining power. Ordinary Face is left facing Linda's invoice. The larger potentiality system is not destroyed, and the ending does not settle anyone else's fate.

Both choices are shown plainly at the terminal. Replay the final room to see the other ending. Neither ending depends on hidden morality points, speed, or undo usage.

## Core loop and controls

Read the room, inspect its signs, preview a word transfer, commit it, and walk through the resulting opening. Borrow the word again when the previous obstacle is behind you. Reach the exit to clear the room. A department transition provides the short story beat before the next puzzle.

| Input | Walking mode | Sign editing mode |
|---|---|---|
| Arrows | Move one floor tile per deliberate press | Select a sign, word plate, tray position, or destination slot |
| Z or A | Open the sign list | Select a plate; then confirm its destination and preview |
| X or B | Undo one committed action | Cancel selection; return to walking mode |
| C | Inspect objects and affected tiles | Inspect the selected sign and its factory state |
| Enter or Start | Pause menu | Pause menu |

The pause menu offers **Resume**, **Undo**, **Restart Room**, **Room Select**, and **Options**. Restart confirms because it discards the current room arrangement. Room Select offers unlocked rooms. No action requires simultaneous buttons, precise timing, a mouse, or typing a sentence.

All registered signs in the current room can be edited remotely. There is no hidden magnet range or line-of-sight restriction. Words are not floor crates; the challenge is their shared use and the route through the room. One selected source, one selected destination, and a visible preview make a transfer explicit.

## Sentence rules

Each object has exactly one registered sign. Its subject and **IS** are fixed. The optional **NOT** plate and the property plate can move.

```text
DOOR A    IS    [NOT]    LOCKED
CAMERA A  IS    [NOT]    ACTIVE
GUARD A   IS    [NOT]    ALERT
BELT A    IS             RIGHT
```

Empty optional brackets mean the sign has no **NOT**. Brackets are interface slots, not words. A printed object label and a connecting highlight show which **DOOR A** or **BELT B** the sign controls. Identically named object types never imply global scope.

| Target | Accepted property | Effect without NOT | Effect with NOT | Factory state in the six rooms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Door | LOCKED | Blocks entry | Allows entry | Locked |
| Camera | ACTIVE | Its marked floor zone causes a violation | Zone is safe | Active |
| Guard | ALERT | Its marked floor zone causes a violation | Zone is safe | Alert |
| Belt | LEFT or RIGHT | Pushes the player one tile in that direction after an action | NOT is not accepted | Left |

**Vocabulary is finite.** The initial six rooms use **NOT**, **LOCKED**, **ACTIVE**, **ALERT**, **LEFT**, and **RIGHT**, plus their fixed subject labels and **IS**. Descriptive wall posters, dialogue, advertisements, and the player's name have no rule effect. No synonyms, arbitrary anagrams, noun transformations, conjunctions, or nested sentences are accepted.

**Every plate is a physical instance.** Moving **NOT** removes it from its source and adds it to its destination in one transaction. Nothing is copied or consumed. An occupied compatible property slot swaps its plate with the source plate. The tray stores unused plates without affecting objects. Two printed **LEFT** plates are two separate instances.

**Incomplete signs use the factory state.** Removing a required property plate turns the sign's link off. The object immediately uses its displayed factory state. It does not retain a previous edited state. This keeps taking **LEFT** off a belt from silently stopping it. The factory state is shown beside every sign and in inspection.

**Incompatible placements cannot commit.** A belt rejects **NOT**; a door rejects **ACTIVE**; an occupied destination rejects a swap if either resulting sign would have an incompatible plate. The preview explains the problem without changing state. There is one sign per target, so contradictory competing signs require no priority rule.

### The first important transfer

Initially:

```text
DOOR A   IS       LOCKED     → locked
CAMERA A IS NOT   ACTIVE     → safe
```

Move the single **NOT** from the camera to the door:

```text
DOOR A   IS NOT   LOCKED     → open
CAMERA A IS       ACTIVE     → hazardous
```

Both changes occur together. Before confirmation, the preview shows the opened door and the newly dangerous camera tiles. A pulse follows the departing word back to its source sign. The source consequence receives as much attention as the destination benefit.

## Movement and consequences

The board is a small orthogonal grid. Walls always block movement. There is no jumping, free flight over walls, pushing other characters, or diagonal shortcut. Each room fits on one screen.

Camera and guard zones are explicit, authored floor tiles. An active object highlights those tiles with a distinct pattern. Cameras do not rotate or track the player; guards do not patrol. Inspection shows the entire zone before the player moves. Their zones remain visible as outlines while inactive.

A committed action resolves in this order:

1. Record the previous complete room state.
2. Apply one valid walking step or one whole word transfer. A blocked walking step changes nothing and creates no undo entry.
3. Evaluate all signs simultaneously and update object states.
4. If the player is on a belt tile, attempt one tile of displacement in its current direction. A wall or locked door blocks that displacement. No second belt displacement occurs during this action.
5. Check the player's final tile against active camera and guard zones.
6. If safe, check whether the player has reached the exit.

A violation freezes play and displays **POLICY VIOLATION** with Undo and Restart. It does not subtract lives, clear a save, or start the room again automatically. Undo reverses the entire action, including a conveyor displacement. Editing a sign can cause a violation if it activates a zone under the player; the preview shows that outcome.

A door may close while the player occupies its tile. The player can leave the occupied tile; subsequent entry is blocked. There is no crush damage or ejection. This rule avoids an invisible timing requirement and remains the same in every room.

An open sign list, inspection, preview, dialogue, or pause menu freezes the simulation. Belts never move on a timer. Holding a movement key cannot conceal several logical actions inside one undo step.

## Six room specifications

These are logical corridor layouts for the first build. The renderer may arrange signs around the corridor, add alcoves for scenery, and give the departments distinct backgrounds. Added walkable routes must preserve the stated puzzle or have their own verified solution.

**Diagram legend:** `S` start; `E` exit; `.` safe floor; `D` controlled door; `g` guard zone; `c` camera zone; `<` belt tile in its factory direction. `S` and `E` are walkable. Diagram cells are numbered from left to right, starting at 1. Above-board signs and decorative camera or guard sprites do not occupy these floor cells. Camera and guard zones may overlap a door or exit, as explicitly specified in Room 6.

### Room 1 The First Decree

**Department:** Customer Entrance. **Lesson:** a word changes a specific object.

```text
S . D . E
```

Door A occupies cell 3. Its sign reads **DOOR A IS LOCKED**. One **NOT** starts in the tray. There are no hazards or belts.

**Solution:** Transfer **NOT** to Door A, then walk to cell 5.

**Checks:** The door visibly opens before walking. Taking **NOT** away closes it. Undo restores the word and door together. An opening line teaches the transfer, then leaves the player to cross.

**Beat:** Dyslexio announces that the superior order has begun. Linda identifies the door as a service entrance.

### Room 2 The Price of Not

**Department:** Customer Entrance. **Lesson:** the source sign changes too.

```text
S . D . c c c . E
```

Door A is cell 3. Camera A's zone is cells 5–7. Initial signs are **DOOR A IS LOCKED** and **CAMERA A IS NOT ACTIVE**. There is exactly one **NOT** and no spare in the tray.

**Solution:** Move **NOT** to Door A. Cross the door and stop at cell 4. Move **NOT** back to Camera A. Walk through cells 5–7 to the exit at 9. Door A closes behind the player.

**Checks:** Moving **NOT** to the door activates the entire camera zone. Cell 4 is safe. The player can still edit the closed door's sign from the far side. Crossing an active zone produces a reversible violation.

**Beat:** The Clerk points out the camera. Dyslexio treats the consequence as a temporary inferior arrangement.

### Room 3 A Word Has Several Jobs

**Department:** Security. **Lesson:** reuse one plate across three obligations.

```text
S . D . g g . D . E
```

Doors A and B are cells 3 and 8. Guard A's zone is cells 5–6. Initial signs are **DOOR A IS LOCKED**, **GUARD A IS NOT ALERT**, and **DOOR B IS LOCKED**. There is one **NOT**.

**Solution:** Borrow **NOT** from the guard to open Door A. Walk to safe cell 4. Return **NOT** to the guard and cross to cell 7. Borrow it again for Door B, then leave at cell 10.

**Checks:** Cells 4 and 7 provide visible safe stopping places. Activating the guard behind the player causes no retrospective penalty. Taking **NOT** from the guard while standing in its zone causes a violation.

**Beat:** A guard's procedural announcement follows its displayed state. Dyslexio declares that even the opposition has accepted his arrangement.

### Room 4 Directed Thought

**Department:** Deliveries. **Lesson:** positive property plates can be replaced.

```text
S . < < < . E
```

Belt A covers cells 3–5. Its sign is **BELT A IS LEFT**. One **RIGHT** starts in the tray. There are no **NOT** plates in this room. The belt's factory state is left.

**Solution:** Swap **RIGHT** into the belt sign. Walk from cell 2 onto cell 3; the belt moves the player to 4. The next eastward step reaches 5 and is displaced to 6. Walk to the exit at 7.

**Checks:** The original **LEFT** moves into the tray. Removing the property entirely leaves the belt running left. Repeated eastward attempts from cell 2 on the unchanged belt return the player to cell 2. Undo restores both the movement and belt displacement.

**Beat:** Dyslexio attributes a reversed delivery belt to superior creative thought. Linda notices a usable improvement to the loading route.

### Room 5 The Delivery Route

**Department:** Deliveries. **Lesson:** coordinate two kinds of borrowed word.

```text
S . < < . c c c . < < . D E
```

Belt A covers cells 3–4; Belt B covers 10–11. Camera A covers cells 6–8. Door A is cell 13. Initial signs are **BELT A IS LEFT**, **BELT B IS LEFT**, **CAMERA A IS NOT ACTIVE**, and **DOOR A IS LOCKED**. One **RIGHT** is in the tray; one **NOT** is on the camera. The two **LEFT** plates are separate instances.

**Solution:** Put **RIGHT** on Belt A. Cross it, then the inactive camera zone, to cell 9. Swap **RIGHT** from Belt A with **LEFT** on Belt B. Cross Belt B to cell 12. Move **NOT** from the camera to Door A, and leave at 14.

**Checks:** Belt A returns left when its **RIGHT** leaves. The camera becomes hazardous only after its zone has been crossed. The transfer preview identifies both belts separately. A solution never needs a key repeat, animation exploit, or duplicate plate.

**Beat:** Linda describes a route optimization. Dyslexio describes the movement of history.

### Room 6 No Further Corrections

**Department:** Policy Archive. **Lesson:** keep the final camera safe while reallocating the other word.

```text
S . D . g g . < < . D c c E
```

Door A is cell 3 and Door B is cell 11. The Spellchecker's guard zone is cells 5–6. Belt A covers cells 8–9. Camera A's zone covers **cells 11–14**, including Door B and the exit; the diagram's final `D` and `E` therefore also carry camera markings.

Initial signs are **DOOR A IS LOCKED**, **GUARD A IS NOT ALERT**, **BELT A IS LEFT**, **DOOR B IS LOCKED**, and **CAMERA A IS NOT ACTIVE**. The two **NOT** plates begin on the guard and camera signs. One **RIGHT** starts in the tray.

**Solution:** Move the guard's **NOT** to Door A and cross to cell 4. Return it to the guard and cross to cell 7. Set Belt A to **RIGHT** and cross to safe cell 10. Move the guard's **NOT** to Door B. Leave the camera's **NOT** in place and cross to cell 14. The terminal then offers the two endings.

**Checks:** The final door opening alone is insufficient if the camera is active. The camera preview highlights Door B and the exit. Earlier doors and guard zones can become dangerous behind the player. Alternate valid arrangements are accepted; the reference sequence is not a required script.

**Beat:** The Spellchecker acknowledges a valid arrangement without conceding Dyslexio's ideology. Linda presents certification as a business decision.

## Campaign scope and expansion

The six rooms form a complete small cartridge with a title, opening, four department settings, finale, two endings, room replay, and credits. Treat room count as a content budget; difficulty and playtime require playtesting. Do not add rooms merely to lengthen the run.

If the first version is readable and enjoyable, a proposed twelve-room edition gives each of the four departments three rooms. Keep the same grammar and object types initially. New room arrangements should create new planning problems before new words are introduced.

| Department | Initial rooms | Expansion direction |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Entrance | 1 and 2 | A return route showing why a borrowed word must come back |
| Security | 3 | Overlapping guard and camera zones; two doors with distinct labels |
| Deliveries | 4 and 5 | A belt route where the safe transfer point is on a side landing |
| Policy Archive | 6 | A rehearsal of simultaneous constraints, then a larger Spellchecker finale |

Expansion relocates and retunes the finale after the preceding rooms. It does not append another unrelated crisis after the ending. Keep a small documented word inventory and at least one verified solution for every added room.

## Presentation and accessibility

Use the collection's **SUPER-16** presentation: 320×224 pixels, the 9-bit palette, crisp integer scaling, and FM music. Arrange a maximum 14×8 floor board using 16-pixel tiles, leaving space for the word tray, controls, selected sign, and preview. Long sign lists scroll; a sentence selected for editing is always shown in full. The logical corridors above fit within that width. [G3]

Reuse the existing Dyslexio portrait and its projected face, helmet, cape, floating letters, and green waveform details. Create a small board sprite from that established look. His movement can hover visually without granting flight mechanics. Preserve the collection's established four-shade treatment for legacy cast portraits such as Linda, while Dyslexio uses his existing colored powered form. [G3, G4]

Give each department a recognizable environment: tiled service entrance, security monitors, delivery channels, and filing cabinets. Keep decoration away from editable signs. The same token frame, target marker, and state patterns work throughout the cartridge.

Word transfers use a short magnetic arc, followed by paired source and destination highlights. Changed sentences remain readable and stationary. A brief villain pose can follow a room clear; the player can advance it immediately. No shaking letters, mirrored instructions, disappearing words, or randomly corrupted text are used to simulate dyslexia.

Use one principal FM theme with department variations, a short transformation sting, and distinct sounds for selection, valid transfer, rejected placement, undo, and violation. Music should leave room for a streamer to discuss the puzzle. Start audio on a user gesture; retain separate music and effect volume controls. Dialogue is readable without speech synthesis.

Accessibility requirements are part of the first build:

- Support keyboard navigation through every menu and sign operation, with a persistent focus marker.
- Pair color with labels, outlines, and floor patterns. A grayscale screenshot must still distinguish hazards, safe tiles, selected words, and open doors.
- Provide a plain text sign list, larger text at the browser level, and readable sentence casing as an option. The rule text does not depend on a stylized font.
- Provide reduced motion, disabling shakes and decorative letter motion while preserving state-change highlights.
- Display dialogue until confirmed. Undo and restart remain available without a reaction-time requirement.
- Offer three optional hints per room: identify the competing objects, identify the word to borrow, then show the reference arrangement sequence. Asking for help never changes the ending.

Implement in the existing **gameboycarl** collection, rather than altering the separate Carl adventure in this workspace. This document is stored with the transcript and character research; the proposed game entry is `J:/games/gameboycarl/dys/index.html`.

The minimum new files are an entry page, `dycore.js` for state and rules, `dyrooms.js` for authored boards, inventories, dialogue, and solutions, `dyart.js` for board art, and `dymain.js` for menus, input flow, music, and persistence. A single `tools/dyscheck.js` check can cover the rule system and supplied solutions. No new dependency, general natural-language parser, or puzzle editor is needed.

Keep object IDs, plate IDs, compatible slots, factory states, floor zones, and the player position explicit in room data. The rule evaluator derives states from the present plate assignments. It must never execute sentence text as code. Imported or saved state is validated against the authored room: unknown plate IDs, duplicate placements, incompatible properties, invalid room IDs, and impossible coordinates are rejected safely.

### Undo and saves

Store a full room-state snapshot before each committed action. The small boards make this simpler than maintaining a second inverse-action system. Restore position, plate assignments, and all action-dependent flags; derive object states again. Transient particles and sound do not belong in the logical state.

Undo is unlimited for the current room while the game is open. A clear banner offers Undo before **Next Room**. Advancing starts a new room history; Room Select restarts an unlocked room from its authored layout.

Use one new local save key, **`dys_out_of_order_save`**, after checking that it is unused in the implementation repository. Save the version, current safe room state, unlocked rooms, and ending flags. Autosave only settled safe states and room clears. Undo history need not survive browser closure; Continue resumes the saved arrangement with a fresh history. State that behavior in the pause help.

Keep saves from other cartridges untouched. Malformed saves offer a fresh start without a crash. When storage is unavailable, play continues for the session and the menu visibly reports that saving is unavailable. The existing storage helper suppresses write errors, so persistence must also verify that the write succeeded before claiming the game was saved. [G5]

## Validation and production order

First implement the sentence evaluator, transfers, grid movement, violation state, undo, and Rooms 1–2 with plain shapes. These rooms must prove that the player notices the lost **NOT** at its source. Next add the guard, belts, and Rooms 3–6. Add character presentation and the ending after the logical puzzles pass. Expansion follows observed playtest results.

A logical walkthrough confirms that all six reference sequences reach their exits safely with the stated **NOT** and **RIGHT** inventories. The document's local source links have also been checked. These checks validate the proposed sequences and references; controls, presentation, and difficulty still require a playable build.

The automated check should verify:

1. Each accepted sentence, missing-property factory behavior, rejected placement, and compatible swap has the specified result.
2. Every transfer conserves the exact plate inventory and updates both targets together.
3. Every reference room solution reaches its exit without a violation. Alternate solutions remain legal.
4. Undo restores complete state after a walking step, transfer, belt displacement, and violation. Restart matches the authored room exactly.
5. Cameras and guards can reactivate behind the player safely, while activation under the player triggers a violation.
6. The final camera covers the door and exit; opening the door while that camera is active does not clear Room 6.
7. Save loading rejects invalid assignments, and storage failure does not prevent play or damage another cartridge's data.

The browser pass should confirm that keyboard focus, sign selection, previews, menus, pause behavior, text scaling, and reduced motion work through an entire run. Reopen a saved game and reach both endings through normal input. Check that hidden tabs and animation timing cannot change puzzle results.

Watch a new player attempt Rooms 1–2 without coaching. They should be able to describe why the door opened, why the camera activated, and how to recover. In later rooms, watch whether they recognize safe stopping tiles and can distinguish identically typed targets. Those observations decide whether to expand, simplify the preview, or redesign a room. The proposed layouts are reference designs, not evidence of tested difficulty.

### Questions to settle through the first build

- Does remote sign editing make the rooms satisfying, or does it remove too much spatial planning? Test the specified version before adding a range restriction.
- Are factory states sufficiently visible when a property plate is removed? If players misread them, improve the display or keep required properties fixed in early rooms.
- Do longer sentences fit comfortably beside the board? Prefer a larger sign view over abbreviations that obscure the rule.
- Do the two endings feel earned after a short campaign? Adjust their dialogue before adding extra systems or hidden conditions.
