# SAVE THE WORLD — a PRETEND CO. gaiden (design)

**SAVE THE WORLD** (SUPER-16, 24 MEGA, battery backup, "WORLD-FX" scaling chip). A side story in the style of a 1994 role-playing epic. It is out of continuity on purpose: the whole cast is recast as fantasy archetypes, the way a gaiden would. The box copy says "THE RPG THAT REMEMBERS." The ad says it is bigger than anything else on any 16-bit system.

## The pitch

Every RPG has a save point. In this one, the save point is the world.

The world of SAVE THE WORLD is a saved game, the SAVE. Its people, towns and memories are kept by three ancient SLOTS (the gods of this world) and by the **SAVE SPIRITS**, beings made of old saved games who live behind the sealed MEMORY CARD gate.

**THE FRANCHISE**, an empire that turns everything into a licensed product, has learned to extract the spirits' power as **SAVE CRYSTALS** and give it to its soldiers. Its court jester, **KURSOR**, is a living menu cursor ▶. Anything he points at gets *selected*. He has seen the title screen, and he wants to select **NEW GAME**: overwrite everything and start the world from scratch, with himself as the only thing that carries over.

At the midpoint he succeeds. The three SLOTS are knocked out of alignment, the world is overwritten, and the second half takes place in the **CORRUPTED SAVE**: everything is four shades of green. Parts of the map are simply gone ("????"), NPCs answer to NEW FILE because they forgot their names, and the party is scattered.

You gather them again, restore what can be restored, and climb Kursor's tower, which is built out of title screens. The last question of the game is the save prompt: **SAVE THE WORLD? YES / NO.**

## The world

### THE SAVE (first half, full color)
- **COLDBOOT**: a snowy mining town where the FIRST SAVE was dug up frozen in the ice. Opening.
- **THE MINES**: escape, plus the HOLD MUSIC (a whelk on permanent hold; hitting the shell is a mistake).
- **FACE CASTLE**: in the desert; it submerges. Kursor sets fire to it.
- **NEVADA**: Carl's desert. The Desert Bus crosses it.
- **THE RETURN**: the rebels' hideout (Returners). Choose to help.
- **GARFEILD**: the diner town JB protects. Kursor deletes its save.
- **THE RERUN EXPRESS**: the ghost train (Spooky Ghost's line).
- **THE PLAINS OF WAITING**: Pee Kid's plain (Veldt analog: every monster type shows up).
- **THE OPERA**: the opera house; Linda sings and Bruce drops weights.
- **THE FACTORY**: Franchise HQ, where save spirits are extracted.
- **THE MEMORY CARD**: the spirits' world, behind the gate.
- **THE MERGER DINNER**: the Franchisor's banquet.
- **THE CLOUD**: the floating continent. Midpoint.

### THE CORRUPTED SAVE (second half, four shades)
- **LOST & FOUND ISLAND**: Linda wakes up. The Clerk raised her for a year.
- **NEW FILE TOWN**: everyone forgot their name.
- The **YOKOID VILLAGE**: Yoko protects the kids.
- **THE FOCUS GROUP**: HU-MAN, secret.
- The **GHOST'S GRAVE**: the second airship, THE RERUN.
- **THE TITLE SCREEN**: Kursor's tower. Final dungeon with three parties, then the tiers.

## Systems

### Field
- 16×16 tiles, characters 16×24, tile-step movement in 4 directions (like the genre).
- A: talk / check; B: cancel / run (held); START: menu; C: TALK (the party's hint).
- Save points glow. The world map has its own save (TENT), as the genre does.
- Treasure chests, hidden items (Carl's FIND on the field: a sparkle).
- Random encounters, counted in steps, per zone.

### Battle (ATB)
- Side view: enemies on the left, the party on the right (up to 4).
- Each fighter has an ATB gauge filled by speed. WAIT mode pauses gauges while you're in a menu (default); ACTIVE is optional.
- Commands: FIGHT (or the kid's verbs), the unique command, MAGIC, ITEM, plus DEFEND/ROW by pressing left/right.
- Damage uses the classic formula (level, power, defense, random variance). Rows: back row halves physical damage both ways.
- Statuses: LAG (poison), MUTE (silence), SCREENSAVER (sleep), SCRAMBLED (confuse), PAUSED (stone), TURBO (haste), BUFFER (slow), CORRUPTED (zombie), MIRROR (reflect), KO.
- DESPERATION: at low HP a character sometimes uses a hidden finisher.
- COMBOS: pairs of commands in sequence fuse.
- AUTO (macro) repeats the last orders.
- Escape: hold B and C together to run.
- Boss scripts: phases, counters, telegraphed moves, dialogue mid-fight.

### Growth
- Levels with experience. HP and MP grow per character curve.
- Equipment: WEAPON, ARMOR, HELM, RELIC×2. Shops in towns.
- SAVE CRYSTALS: one per character. While it's equipped, after a battle the character learns its spells (progress %). Level-up bonuses come from the crystal (like espers). Summon once per battle.

### Spells (the sorcery is named like settings)
HOT / HOTTER / HOTTEST, COLD / COLDER / COLDEST, ZAP / ZAPPER / ZAPPIEST, BETTER / BETTERER / BEST (cures), CONTINUE (revive), LAG, MUTE, SCREENSAVER, SCRAMBLE, TURBO, BUFFER, MIRROR MODE, RUMBLE (quake), DRAIN, PAUSE (stone), LEVITATE, CREDITS (meteor: the credits roll and hit everything), THE END (ultima).

## Endings
- **SAVE THE WORLD? YES**: the epilogue, character by character; color comes back from the edges of the screen.
- **NO**: "THE WORLD WAS NOT SAVED. IT CONTINUES ANYWAY." A short ending where everyone goes on in four shades, together. It's not a bad ending.
- Secret: everyone recruited, HU-MAN found, and Pilot X waited for → an extra scene after the credits.

## Content rules
- The gaiden is out of continuity. The cast keep their personalities and voices from the bibles.
- TP is outside the lore and is not in this game, except maybe as a cartridge.
- Pee Kid never uses violence. His verbs are asking, performing and inspecting.
- Deadpan, in-world text. Short lines.

## Saves
- `stw_save` (three slots: SLOT 1, SLOT 2, SLOT 3 — the three gods of the world) and `stw_meta` (endings, play count).
- Reads the other games' metas for small touches (cameos in town; the Clerk mentions things he's found).
