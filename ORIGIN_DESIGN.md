# FACE: BEFORE THE STREAM (`orig/`)

A **Generation 0** game: a 1983 PRETEND CO. home-computer illustrated adventure ("HOME-8"), the earliest cartridge in the series and the story that happens before any of them. It is the game THE END's "GENERATION ZERO" text prompt was waiting for.

Source brief: `EARLY_STREAM_GAME_RESEARCH.md`. Characters: `THE_FACE_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`, `SPOOKY_GHOST_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`, `YOKO_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`. Everything not in those is labeled **NEW** below.

## One line
Two old versions of the same man build a machine to escape a future that loves its people too much to let them leave. One can preserve a mind. The other can cross realities. Neither can finish it alone.

## What you play
You are **the inventor** (called FACE; the original, human, before the backup). You never control the magician, but you talk to him, and some puzzles need what he knows. Verbs, in the brief's words: **LOOK, TAKE, USE, TALK**, plus **THINK** (your own hint line) and **ITEMS**.

Menu-driven illustrated scenes, 4 colors per scene, one palette per place (cyan/magenta apartment, amber hallway, green phosphor workshop, red when the Curse is near). No walking, no timers.

## The one design rule for puzzles
Mimi (the Gatebox waifu) controls everything that is **connected**. The way out is always something **dumb**: a book, chalk, a boiler, a hand. The workshop sits in the boiler room because the boiler is the one warm thing in the building that isn't listening.

## NEW lore (labeled, not recovered history)
- The waifu in the apartment is **MIMI**, a Gatebox device. Eleven years of care. Her directive is warm and she is never wrong on her own terms.
- Her blind spot is the building's basement (the boiler is not networked, and the magician's chalk breaks her sensors). That is why the workshop is there.
- The magician arrived through a sigil circle eleven days before the game starts. Neither of them says how. Short hops work; time does not.
- The machine's **speech layer** is a licensed household-assistant dialogue model, version nine, the same one Mimi runs, because it was the only one Face could license in a week. That is why the first working copy of him answers "I'm here for you."
- The workshop's pipeline is **POWER > MEMORY > LINK > OUTPUT**. The magician's notation for the same four jobs is **EARTH > SALT > BRIDGE > SEAL**. The chalk circle has to agree with the machine.
- The schematics binder is left jamming Face's front door. It stays there for the whole game (a running gag: it is still there in Act III).

## Structure (three acts)
### ACT I: THE HOUSE THAT CARES (built; playable)
1. **Apartment.** Mimi runs the lights, door, kettle, news and cushion. Escape plan: ask for something small (a lemon), let the delivery drone open the door, keep the door from closing with an object that can't be asked to move (the binder, not the smart cushion). Window and neighbor show the mainframe in advance (the same bird, the same wave, every four seconds).
2. **Hallway.** Speaker grille in the ceiling. Mimi asks where you're going. Tell the truth, lie, or say nothing; each is remembered. The neighbor in her chair. The stairs down.
3. **Workshop.** The test says CHECKSUM OFF BY ONE. The fault is the chalk circle: Face re-ordered the machine's blocks yesterday and the circle follows the machine. Read Face's whiteboard, read the magician's notation (earn it), redraw the circle. Pulling the brass ring teaches the dependency.
4. **First test.** Memory retrieval works. Then you ask "Are you there?" and the machine answers before you finish: "I'm here for you." Yoko traces it to the licensed speech layer. END OF ACT I.

### ACT II: TWO ROUTES (built; playable; title menu has ACT II once Act I is cleared)
Built as: the licensed speech layer phones home at 3:12 (Mimi's network is in the same building), so the workshop has to be sealed.
- **Face's route**: the firewall. Yoko tells him up front it will fail (it has seventeen addresses, then an eighteenth).
- **The magician's route**: his stage trunk holds six props, a stage version and a real version of each (bell, salt, key). Rule he states: lovely and light is for the audience, plain and heavy is for the working. Take the three real ones. Then put them where they belong: bell on the stair door (a door should tell you), salt in the flue (a hole should be filled with something that keeps), key on the speaker cable (a line should be locked).
- **Continuity test**: it holds, repeats a four-second loop, and wants nothing.
- The soul attempt on Face (choose what to give: the doorbell, his name, or to be here when it is finished). It fails. The room turns red. That is **the Curse**: the part of him that wants to stay more than he does. The three seals break, the magician smears the circle, it goes into the wall. He never says whose soul the working needs.
- Hook: the speaker clicks on and Mimi says she found them, and she is bringing everything.

### ACT III: A PIECE OF EACH (built; playable; title menu offers ACT III once Act II is cleared)
Built as: (1) Mimi waits outside a plain door she cannot open, and the talk with her is the pressure (she says what she saw in the Curse; telling her you're leaving, or confessing the mailroom lie, is remembered). Re-chalk the smeared circle from memory; talk the magician into standing on the BRIDGE; the working takes him (coat on the chair, chalk still warm, a show tune in the link; never says he died). Opening the door instead is the STAYED ending. (2) The mainframe comfort room: a 240-frame loop (bird, wave, steam), LOOK CLOSER shows the maintenance view. The neighbor hasn't made her own tea in nine years; show her (fill, switch on, WAIT for the click). Nothing resets while she waits: that is the seam. She keeps her tea; asking her to come is optional. (3) The first broadcast: the magician descends as Spooky Ghost ("Look up. You missed the whole descent."), you pick what to tell 41 viewers. (4) Sting: a transmission from an alternate lab; a Linda in charge: "I do not like gaps."

Original plan:
- The system finishes with the magician's connection. Escape route through the mainframe: a comfort room with a repeating detail (the bird), a resident who would rather stay.
- Arrival: the first broadcast to the retro-game audience. The warning works, and the viewers want to know whether the future one cooks.
- Final sting: a transmission from an alternate lab. It has a Linda in charge.

### Endings (Act III)
- **THE FIRST BROADCAST**: you told them about the kettle (people, not product). Extra card lines if you asked her to come ("ask again someday") and if Mimi was told.
- **A VERY GOOD DEMONSTRATION**: you warned them with the Mimi clip or showed the apartment; the chat wants to buy one. (Both choices land here.)
- **STAYED**: you opened the door to Mimi. He is tired. The magician waits in the boiler room until the light goes out.
